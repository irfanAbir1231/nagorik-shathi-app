# Audit of the Jev evidence architecture — 2026-09-30

**Subject:** commit `663a8d5` on `feat/jev-semantic-evidence` ("Add Jev semantic evidence layer…").
**Method:** adversarial — every claim of the previous report was re-tested, and every suspected defect was reproduced before being listed. Probes are in `probes/` (see "Reproducing"). Numbers below are exactly as measured on that commit, **before** any of the fixes that follow it; later results live in `evaluation/results/jev-latest.md`.

## 1. The finding that changed the thesis

The original evaluation compared Jev with the pre-existing DeepSeek "civic frame" prompt, which had received no design work. Re-running DeepSeek on the **same typed questions, thresholds and policy** (an LLM behind the same `DecisionModel` interface; `probes/same-questions-llm.ts`):

| Engine (same questions & policy unless noted) | Precision | Recall | False assertions | Held for confirmation | e2e false "eligible" | e2e positive recall | ECE (asserted facts) | Bangla recall | p50 latency |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| DeepSeek, original civic-frame prompt | 75.4% | 65.0% | 23 | — | 6 | 55.7% | 0.209 | 63% | 1,133 ms |
| DeepSeek, same typed questions | 97.6% | 76.9% | 3 | 52 | 2 | 93.4% | 0.045 | 79% | 2,657 ms |
| Jev (jev-1.13.0) | 99.3% | 91.9% | 1 | 24 | 0 | 90.2% | 0.055 | 96% | 320 ms |

Corrections to the previous report:

- Most of the improvement comes from the **architecture** (typed questions about the writer, explicit "not stated", code-owned values, acceptance policy), not from Jev specifically.
- "The LLM's confidence is too miscalibrated to route on" was overstated: that was the old prompt's self-reported `confidence` field. Under the typed-question format DeepSeek's probabilities were at least as well calibrated as Jev's on this set.
- Jev's advantage is real but narrower: 8.3× lower median latency, half the confirmation prompts, higher Bangla recall (96% vs 79%), fewer false assertions (1 vs 3) and false "eligible" verdicts (0 vs 2).

## 3. Evaluation validity

- **Contamination:** 17 of 109 semantic cases have a near-duplicate (bigram Jaccard ≥ 0.30; 5 exact) among the 18 probe messages used while designing the questions (`probes/contamination.ts`). On the 92 uncontaminated cases: Jev precision 99.2%, recall 92.3%, 1 false assertion, 0 false "eligible"; DeepSeek-same-questions 97.1% / 76.9% / 3 / 2. The conclusions hold; the headline set should still be reported as partly dev.
- **Single author-annotator**, wide intervals: 6 injection positives, 14 held-out verifier pairs.
- **Concentration:** the regex arm's false "eligible" verdicts are dominated by one permissive programme (government legal aid).
- **Tested and rejected:** accepting a fact only when Jev and DeepSeek agree. DeepSeek corroborated 150/150 Jev-accepted facts (including Jev's one labelled error, `d-neg-en`, which both engines read as "unemployed" — consistent with it being a labelling call). No gain on this set.

## 4. What survived

- 0 false "eligible" verdicts for the Jev arm on both the full and the uncontaminated set.
- The architecture is model-agnostic: a second engine behind the same contract also stays at ≤ 3 false assertions.
- The verifier resisted all five steering attempts and caught every labelled contradiction; every failure path falls back to the deterministic template.
- The API key never leaves the server; the whole study reruns offline from the cache.

## 5. Improvements agreed from this audit

1. Check every number in the prose against the right programme (defects 1, 2).
2. Replace the regex fallback with the typed-question LLM: Jev → typed LLM → regex held for confirmation.
3. Fix the confirmation flow: resolve conflicts in chat, confirm facts one by one (defects 3, 4).
4. Make the evaluation independent: second annotator, blind real anonymised messages, agreement scores, confidence intervals.
5. Version the engine; sign-off on the three programmes whose verdicts moved (defect 5).
6. A deadline on each turn (defect 7).
7. Harden the verifier: reviewer-addressed text; caveats for unverified sample data (defect 6).
8. Pin each fact to the part of the message it came from.
9. Record explicit denials instead of discarding them.
10. Verify five flagship programmes against current government circulars.
11. Redact personal details before vendor calls; stop storing extracted facts in plain text in AI logs.

### New findings while fixing

- **The typed LLM breaks JSON on about 4% of turns.** 5 of 116 turns collapsed a Noul answer to `{"type":"noul":0.05}`. `repairJsonSyntax` rewrites exactly that pattern, which cannot occur in valid JSON; 1 turn (an extra closing brace) still fails and falls back to held patterns, which is safe.
- **The evaluation harness was stricter than production.** After fix 1, three clean-knowledge turns hit the numeric gate for repeating the citizen's own income. The harness plan omitted the accepted facts that `runTurn` attaches. With parity restored, 14/16 clean turns show model prose; the 2 rejected turns called an unverified programme eligible with no caution, which is fix 6 working.
- **The typed-LLM threshold is on a knife edge.** 0.91 meets the 97% target at the 95% lower bound on all cases but not on the clean split, and the difference is one case. 0.90 is kept (`policy.ts`, `ENGINE_THRESHOLD_SOURCE`).
- **The new caution check was too broad on clarification turns** (found in the end-to-end demo, `npm run demo:jev` on a local database, not by the study). It demanded a "confirm first" caution for every eligible unverified programme in the plan, even when the prose only asked "which marital status is right?". Verifier 1.2.1 skips the caution when Jev reads the text as not mentioning that programme (P ≥ 0.5; a missing reading keeps the check on). It changed no result in the study; the two rejected study turns do present the programme. In the demo, both previously rejected turns then passed.
- **Old AI-log rows are not rewritten.** Rows written before this change still hold extracted facts in plain text. This needs a one-off purge or re-seal on the production database, which was not touched here.
- **The fixes cost tokens and latency.** Jev input grew from 2,542 to 3,721 tokens per turn (span questions, three-way flags): $0.000107 → $0.000156 per turn at list price. p95 rose from 489 to 587 ms. Jev accuracy on question set 2.0.0 matched 1.0.0 (precision 99.4% vs 99.3%; 1 false assertion in both; negation-category recall 80% vs 67%).
